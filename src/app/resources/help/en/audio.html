Sound

In the Audio tab you clean up the sound of the selected clip and set the loudness of the whole mix. Sound changes never re-encode the picture.

  • 1 Clip sound
  • 2 Equaliser (scroll down for the compressor, ducking and the master)

The clip's sound

Select a clip with sound on the timeline, then:

  • Volume (dB): make the clip louder or quieter.
  • Low cut (Hz): removes rumble, wind and handling noise. 80 Hz is a good start for speech.
  • Noise reduction (%): lowers steady noise such as hum, hiss, fans and air conditioning. Very high values can sound watery.
  • Equaliser: Bass, Middle and Treble.
  • Compressor: evens out loud and quiet parts so a voice stays at one level.
  • Ducking: turn it on for music; it gets quieter by itself while another clip has speech.

Master (whole mix)

  • 1 Compressor
  • 2 Ducking
  • 3 Master (whole mix)
  • Normalise loudness on export: measures the finished mix and brings it to the target. Choose -14 LUFS for YouTube, Spotify and social media, -16 LUFS for podcasts, -23 LUFS for television.
  • Limiter: stops peaks from going above the Ceiling, so nothing clips.
  • Measure loudness: shows the mix's current loudness and peak.

Sound in the Edit tab

For a quick change, Properties → Audio → Volume in Edit works too. To silence a whole audio track, press the speaker button in its header.

To silence only some clips, select them on the timeline (Ctrl-click for several) and press M, or right-click and choose Mute clip sound. The picture stays, only the sound goes quiet, and the clip shows Muted. Press M again to bring the sound back. The other clips on the same track keep their sound.
